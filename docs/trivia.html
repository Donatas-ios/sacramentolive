🎲 Sacramento Trivia

Fun facts and history about California's capital city

🏛️

Capital City

Sacramento has been California's state capital since 1854
Before Sacramento, California's capital moved around — San Jose, Vallejo, and Benicia all served as capital before Sacramento was made permanent.
⛏️

Gold Rush

The California Gold Rush of 1848 put Sacramento on the map
Sacramento became the main supply hub for the 300,000+ miners who flooded California. John Sutter's Fort was the first non-indigenous settlement in the area, established in 1839.
🌳

Tree City

Sacramento is known as the "City of Trees"
Sacramento has one of the largest urban forests in the world, with over 1 million trees lining its streets. The city receives more tree canopy coverage than Paris or London.
🚂

Transcontinental Railroad

Sacramento was the western terminus of the First Transcontinental Railroad
In 1863, Sacramento was chosen as the starting point for the Central Pacific Railroad. The railroad was completed in 1869, connecting the country coast to coast.
🌾

Farm to Fork Capital

Sacramento is America's Farm-to-Fork Capital
Located in the Sacramento Valley, the region produces over 300 crops. Within 150 miles of Sacramento, you can find more food diversity than almost anywhere else in the world.
🏀

Sacramento Kings

The Sacramento Kings are the NBA's oldest franchise in continuous operation
Founded in 1923 as the Rochester Seagrams, the Kings moved to Sacramento in 1985 and play at the Golden 1 Center, one of the most technologically advanced arenas in the world.
🌊

River City

Sacramento sits at the confluence of two major rivers
The Sacramento River and American River meet just west of downtown. The city's extensive levee system protects it from flooding — Sacramento is one of the most flood-prone cities in America.
☀️

Sunny City

Sacramento averages 265 sunny days per year
Sacramento is one of the sunniest cities in the United States, sunnier than Los Angeles or Miami. Summers are hot and dry, with temperatures regularly exceeding 100°F.
🏘️

Midtown & Victorian Homes

Sacramento's Poverty Ridge neighborhood has one of California's best collections of Victorian homes
The area around 16th Street and H Street (nicknamed "Poverty Ridge" ironically for its wealthy residents) contains hundreds of beautifully preserved Victorian mansions from the 1800s.
🎭

Pony Express

Sacramento was the western terminus of the Pony Express
From 1860 to 1861, the Pony Express delivered mail between Sacramento and St. Joseph, Missouri in just 10 days. The service was made obsolete by the telegraph in October 1861.
🌉

Tower Bridge

Sacramento's Tower Bridge is one of only four vertical-lift bridges in California
Opened in 1935, the golden Tower Bridge connects Sacramento to West Sacramento and is a beloved landmark. It lifts vertically to allow tall ships to pass through to the Port of Sacramento.
🍅

Tomato Capital

The Sacramento Valley produces about 90% of the nation's processed tomatoes
California grows more tomatoes than any other state, and the Sacramento Valley is the heart of that production. The region also leads in almonds, rice, and many other crops.

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

1. What year did Sacramento become California's permanent state capital?

Sacramento became the permanent capital in 1854 after the legislature grew tired of moving between San Jose, Vallejo, and Benicia.

2. What nickname is Sacramento known by because of its trees?

Sacramento's urban forest of over 1 million trees earns it the nickname "City of Trees" — with more canopy coverage per capita than most cities in the world.

3. Which two rivers meet near downtown Sacramento?

The Sacramento River and American River converge just west of downtown, making Sacramento a true "River City."

4. What percentage of America's processed tomatoes come from the Sacramento Valley?

The Sacramento Valley produces about 90% of all processed tomatoes in the United States, making it the undisputed tomato capital of America.

5. In what year was the Pony Express mail service replaced by the telegraph?

The Pony Express operated from April 1860 to October 1861, when the transcontinental telegraph made it obsolete. It lasted just 18 months.
