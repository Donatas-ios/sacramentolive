🎲 Sacramento Trivia

Daily selection of fun facts about California's capital · Updated September 6, 2026

🦆

Wildlife

The Sacramento Valley hosts one of the largest waterfowl migrations in North America
Every winter, millions of ducks, geese, and shorebirds travel the Pacific Flyway through the Sacramento Valley. The region has some of the best bird watching in the country.
🌾

Farm to Fork Capital

Sacramento is America's Farm-to-Fork Capital
Located in the Sacramento Valley, the region produces over 300 crops. Within 150 miles, you can find more food diversity than almost anywhere else in the world.
🎪

State Fair

The California State Fair has been held in Sacramento every year since 1854
Cal Expo hosts the California State Fair each summer, drawing nearly 500,000 visitors. It's one of the largest state fairs in the US, featuring agriculture, entertainment, and food.
🚀

Technology

Sacramento is one of the fastest-growing tech hubs outside Silicon Valley
With lower costs than the Bay Area and proximity to UC Davis, Sacramento has attracted major tech companies and startups. Apple, Google, and Intel all have operations nearby.
🍅

Tomato Capital

The Sacramento Valley produces about 90% of the nation's processed tomatoes
California grows more tomatoes than any other state, and the Sacramento Valley leads that production. The region also dominates in almonds, rice, and stone fruits.
🌳

Tree City

Sacramento is known as the 'City of Trees'
Sacramento has one of the largest urban forests in the world, with over 1 million trees lining its streets — more canopy coverage per capita than Paris or London.
🏗️

Old Sacramento

Old Sacramento is a 28-acre National Historic Landmark
Old Sacramento preserves the look of the 1850s Gold Rush era with wooden sidewalks, brick buildings, and cobblestone streets along the Sacramento River waterfront.
🚂

Transcontinental Railroad

Sacramento was the western terminus of the First Transcontinental Railroad
In 1863, Sacramento was chosen as the starting point for the Central Pacific Railroad. The railroad was completed in 1869, connecting the country coast to coast for the first time.

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

1. How many sunny days does Sacramento average per year?

Sacramento averages 265 sunny days per year — sunnier than Miami and one of the sunniest large cities in the US.

2. Which two rivers meet near downtown Sacramento?

The Sacramento River and American River converge just west of downtown, making Sacramento a true River City.

3. What year did Sacramento Republic FC set a USL attendance world record?

In 2014, Sacramento Republic FC drew over 11,000 fans to their USL home opener — a world record for the league at the time.

4. What is tule fog?

Tule fog is a dense ground fog that forms in the Sacramento Valley in winter. It can reduce visibility to near zero and is the leading cause of weather-related accidents in California.

5. What is the oldest public art museum in the American West?

The Crocker Art Museum, founded in 1885 and donated to Sacramento by Judge Edwin Crocker, is the oldest public art museum in the western United States.
