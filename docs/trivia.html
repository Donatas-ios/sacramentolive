🎲 Sacramento Trivia

Daily selection of fun facts about California's capital · Updated June 10, 2026

🍷

Wine Country

The Sacramento region has more American Viticultural Areas than Napa Valley
The Sierra Foothills wine region surrounding Sacramento has over 100 wineries. El Dorado, Amador, and Calaveras counties are renowned for Zinfandel and Syrah.
⛏️

Gold Rush

The California Gold Rush of 1848 put Sacramento on the map
Sacramento became the main supply hub for the 300,000+ miners who flooded California. John Sutter's Fort was the first non-indigenous settlement in the area, established in 1839.
🌉

Tower Bridge

Sacramento's Tower Bridge is one of only four vertical-lift bridges in California
Opened in 1935, the golden Tower Bridge connects Sacramento to West Sacramento. It lifts vertically to allow tall ships to pass through to the Port of Sacramento.
🌿

Parks

William Land Park has been a community gathering place since 1918
The 160-acre William Land Park in south Sacramento includes a golf course, Funderland amusement park, Sacramento Zoo, and William Land Regional Park — all free or low-cost.
🏥

Healthcare

UC Davis Medical Center in Sacramento is one of the top trauma centers on the West Coast
The UC Davis Medical Center is the only Level 1 trauma center in the region serving a 33-county area. It's also home to the UC Davis Comprehensive Cancer Center.
📚

Education

Sacramento State was founded in 1947 and is the only university in the California State system named after a city
California State University, Sacramento (Sac State) serves over 30,000 students and is a major economic driver for the region, contributing over $2 billion annually to the local economy.
🎨

Arts

The Crocker Art Museum is the oldest public art museum in the American West
Founded in 1885, the Crocker Art Museum houses an impressive collection of Californian, European, and Asian art. It was donated to the city by Judge Edwin Crocker.
🎸

Music

Sacramento has produced major music acts including Tesla, Deftones, and Cake
The Sacramento music scene punches above its weight. Deftones formed at Del Campo High School in the 1980s and went on to global fame with alternative metal.

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

1. How many sunny days does Sacramento average per year?

Sacramento averages 265 sunny days per year — sunnier than Miami and one of the sunniest large cities in the US.

2. What year did Sacramento become California's permanent state capital?

Sacramento became the permanent capital in 1854 after the legislature grew tired of moving between San Jose, Vallejo, and Benicia.

3. What is the oldest public art museum in the American West?

The Crocker Art Museum, founded in 1885 and donated to Sacramento by Judge Edwin Crocker, is the oldest public art museum in the western United States.

4. Which Sacramento band is known for alternative metal hits like 'Change'?

Deftones formed at Del Campo High School in Sacramento in the 1980s and became globally renowned for their unique blend of alternative metal and shoegaze.

5. When was Old Sacramento designated a National Historic Landmark?

Old Sacramento was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1965, preserving 28 acres of Gold Rush-era buildings along the Sacramento River waterfront.
