🎲 Sacramento Trivia

Daily selection of fun facts about California's capital · Updated June 9, 2026

⛏️

Gold Rush

The California Gold Rush of 1848 put Sacramento on the map
Sacramento became the main supply hub for the 300,000+ miners who flooded California. John Sutter's Fort was the first non-indigenous settlement in the area, established in 1839.
🌿

Parks

William Land Park has been a community gathering place since 1918
The 160-acre William Land Park in south Sacramento includes a golf course, Funderland amusement park, Sacramento Zoo, and William Land Regional Park — all free or low-cost.
🌮

Food Scene

Sacramento has more restaurants per capita than San Francisco
The combination of fresh local produce, diverse immigrant communities, and a thriving food culture makes Sacramento one of the best eating cities in California.
🍅

Tomato Capital

The Sacramento Valley produces about 90% of the nation's processed tomatoes
California grows more tomatoes than any other state, and the Sacramento Valley leads that production. The region also dominates in almonds, rice, and stone fruits.
🚂

Transcontinental Railroad

Sacramento was the western terminus of the First Transcontinental Railroad
In 1863, Sacramento was chosen as the starting point for the Central Pacific Railroad. The railroad was completed in 1869, connecting the country coast to coast for the first time.
🏗️

Old Sacramento

Old Sacramento is a 28-acre National Historic Landmark
Old Sacramento preserves the look of the 1850s Gold Rush era with wooden sidewalks, brick buildings, and cobblestone streets along the Sacramento River waterfront.
🏛️

Capital City

Sacramento has been California's state capital since 1854
Before Sacramento, California's capital moved around — San Jose, Vallejo, and Benicia all served as capital before Sacramento was made permanent.
📚

Education

Sacramento State was founded in 1947 and is the only university in the California State system named after a city
California State University, Sacramento (Sac State) serves over 30,000 students and is a major economic driver for the region, contributing over $2 billion annually to the local economy.

🧠 Test Your Knowledge

1. What year did Sacramento become California's permanent state capital?

Sacramento became the permanent capital in 1854 after the legislature grew tired of moving between San Jose, Vallejo, and Benicia.

2. What is tule fog?

Tule fog is a dense ground fog that forms in the Sacramento Valley in winter. It can reduce visibility to near zero and is the leading cause of weather-related accidents in California.

3. What is the oldest public art museum in the American West?

The Crocker Art Museum, founded in 1885 and donated to Sacramento by Judge Edwin Crocker, is the oldest public art museum in the western United States.

4. What nickname is Sacramento known by because of its trees?

Sacramento's urban forest of over 1 million trees earns it the nickname 'City of Trees' — with more canopy per capita than most cities worldwide.

5. How many counties does UC Davis Medical Center serve as a trauma center?

UC Davis Medical Center is the only Level 1 trauma center serving a 33-county area in Northern California and Nevada.
